# Performative Optimization

A compact, reproducible research benchmark for **operations research under decision-dependent data distributions**. The repository studies a synthetic service-capacity / inventory-style newsvendor problem in which the deployed capacity changes future demand. The resulting feedback loop makes the data-generating distribution endogenous to the decision.

This is an independent implementation informed by the performative prediction, stochastic optimization, and decision-dependent uncertainty literature. It is **not** a reproduction of a paper's code and does not claim paper-level benchmark performance.

## Motivation

Classical predict-then-optimize assumes that deploying the decision does not change the distribution used for future prediction:

```text
context / historical data -> prediction -> optimization decision
```

The performative setting is different:

```text
data -> prediction / decision -> environment response
     -> future data distribution -> new decision -> feedback loop
```

In industrial systems, capacity, price, inventory, staffing, service level, and supplier-allocation decisions can affect later demand, availability, customer retention, congestion, or response behavior. The benchmark isolates this mechanism in a model that is simple enough to verify analytically but rich enough to distinguish stability from decision quality.

## Research Question

The benchmark asks:

1. How much decision quality is lost when a standard predict-then-optimize policy ignores decision-induced distribution shift?
2. Does repeated re-optimization settle down, and if it does, is the resulting self-consistent decision also good under **performative risk**?
3. Can an explicitly learned response map improve downstream decisions?
4. How do stronger feedback effects change performative risk, stability residual, decision oscillation, and distribution shift?
5. What deployment, sampling, optimization, and fixed-point-computation budgets does each method consume?

The central methodological distinction is that **performative stability is not the same object as performative-risk minimization**. Miller, Perdomo, and Zrnic (2021) show that stable solutions can be far from minimizers of performative risk.

## Mathematical Problem

The decision is a scalar capacity level

\[
q \in [q_{\min}, q_{\max}].
\]

After deploying \(q\), demand is generated by the decision-dependent distribution map

\[
\mathcal D(q):\qquad D(q) \sim \mathcal N(\mu(q),\sigma^2),
\qquad \mu(q)=\mu_0+\alpha q.
\]

Here \(\alpha\) controls performative sensitivity. Positive \(\alpha\) means that deploying more capacity increases future demand, for example because availability or service quality attracts or retains demand.

For realized demand \(d\), cost is

\[
\ell(q,d)
= c q + c_u(d-q)_+ + c_o(q-d)_+,
\]

where \(c\) is capacity cost, \(c_u\) shortage cost, and \(c_o\) overage cost.

For a frozen Normal demand distribution with mean \(\mu\), the classical OR risk is

\[
R_{\text{static}}(q;\mu)
= \mathbb E_{D\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)}[\ell(q,D)].
\]

The decision-relevant performative risk is

\[
R_{\text{perf}}(q)
= \mathbb E_{D\sim\mathcal D(q)}[\ell(q,D)].
\]

The repository reports both, but treats \(R_{\text{perf}}\) as the primary decision-quality metric.

### Classical newsvendor optimizer

For a fixed distribution, an interior optimizer satisfies

\[
F_D(q^*)=\frac{c_u-c}{c_u+c_o}.
\]

For Normal demand this gives an analytical candidate, after which the feasible endpoints are checked. This exact one-dimensional newsvendor solve is the classical OR decision engine used by the static and repeated-optimization baselines.

### Performatively stable decision

Given an estimated response map \(\widehat\mu(q)\), the stable method solves the fixed-point condition

\[
q_{\text{stable}}
= \arg\min_u R_{\text{static}}(u;\widehat\mu(q_{\text{stable}})).
\]

The implementation finds the zero of the corresponding best-response residual and verifies the residual after solving.

### Known-response oracle

For the synthetic affine-Normal environment, the performative risk is globally convex in \(q\). Writing \(\mu(q)=a+bq\), its second derivative is proportional to

\[
(1-b)^2\frac{\phi((q-\mu(q))/\sigma)}{\sigma}(c_u+c_o)\ge 0.
\]

The stationary condition therefore yields an analytical global candidate; the implementation compares it with both feasible endpoints. This gives a **known-response analytical oracle for this synthetic model**. It is independently checked in tests against SciPy bounded numerical optimization and a dense grid.

This exactness does not extend automatically to general performative optimization problems.

## Methodology

Five methods are implemented:

1. **Naive static / standard predict-then-optimize.** Observe demand at the initial deployment, estimate its mean, solve one frozen-distribution newsvendor problem, and ignore the induced shift thereafter.
2. **Repeated optimization.** Repeatedly deploy the current capacity, sample the induced demand, re-estimate its mean, and solve a new frozen-distribution newsvendor problem. The feedback loop is executed in code rather than merely described in the README.
3. **Performatively stable method.** Collect intervention data, fit a linear response map, and solve its self-consistent best-response fixed point.
4. **Plug-in performative optimization.** Fit the same response map, insert it into the decision-dependent risk, and minimize the modeled performative risk directly.
5. **Known-response analytical oracle.** Use the true synthetic response map to compute the globally minimizing decision for the benchmark instance.

The learned component is deliberately narrow: it estimates the environment response \(q\mapsto\mu(q)\). It does **not** learn the OR feasibility region, cost function, or optimization algorithm.

## Baselines

The benchmark uses interpretable OR baselines rather than a weak random comparator:

- exact frozen-distribution Normal newsvendor optimization;
- one-shot predict-then-optimize;
- repeated re-estimation and re-optimization;
- a performatively stable fixed point;
- direct model-based performative-risk minimization;
- a true-response analytical oracle available only because the environment is synthetic and one-dimensional.

A new method is not assumed to beat these baselines. Negative paired differences, null differences, and cases where repeated optimization performs better are valid outcomes.

## Prediction Accuracy vs Decision Quality

Prediction quality and decision quality are not equivalent. A response model is useful here only insofar as its induced decision has low performative risk. For that reason predictive MSE is not the headline metric.

Two response models with similar prediction error can imply different decisions near an optimization threshold, while a modest response-model error can be decision-irrelevant if the induced optimizer is insensitive in that region. The benchmark therefore evaluates final decisions under the true synthetic environment.

## Evaluation Protocol

The default configuration uses ten fixed seeds. All methods for a seed share the same initial demand-noise realization where that comparison is meaningful.

Reported metrics include:

- **performative risk** under the distribution induced by the final decision;
- **regret to the known-response oracle**;
- **naive/static risk** under the initially estimated frozen distribution;
- **stability residual** under the true evaluation environment;
- **decision oscillation** for iterative deployment;
- **distribution shift magnitude**, equal here to the 1-Wasserstein distance between equal-variance Normal distributions;
- **feasibility and total bound violation**;
- **deployments, demand samples, optimization calls, and fixed-point evaluations**.

Across seeds, the benchmark reports mean, sample standard deviation, median, p90, and a two-sided 95% Student-t confidence interval. It also reports paired risk differences against both `naive_static` and `repeated_optimization`. Negative paired differences mean the first method has lower performative risk.

### Calibration / test separation

This version does not tune a high-capacity ML model, so there is no validation-set hyperparameter search. The response map is estimated from a separate intervention/calibration RNG stream, while final decision quality is evaluated analytically under the known synthetic distribution.

The test seeds are not used to select model classes or optimization hyperparameters. If richer learned response models are added later, train/validation/test model selection should be explicit rather than folded into the benchmark loop.

### OOD protocol

OOD evaluation increases the true performative sensitivity from the nominal \(\alpha\) to `ood_sensitivity`.

Crucially, the response model is **calibrated only on the nominal environment and is not re-fit after the OOD shift**. This makes the OOD split a genuine response-model misspecification test. Repeated optimization may adapt through new deployments; the plug-in and stable methods keep the nominal learned response map.

The same seed is used for nominal and OOD runs so the Gaussian noise realization is paired across the two environments.

## Exactness / Verification

Exactness and approximation are separated explicitly:

- analytical Normal expectations are used for shortage and overage cost;
- the frozen-distribution newsvendor solve is analytical plus endpoint checks;
- the known-response oracle is an analytical global solve for the affine-Normal benchmark;
- SciPy bounded optimization and a dense grid provide independent numerical checks of the oracle;
- the performatively stable method solves a numerical scalar fixed point and verifies its residual;
- the response map is estimated from finite samples and is therefore approximate;
- repeated optimization is sample-based and therefore stochastic;
- every returned decision is independently audited and its objective is recomputed.

The term **global optimum** is used only for the analytical oracle within this stated synthetic model. No such claim is made for general performative optimization.

## Feasibility Audit

Every decision is checked independently against

\[
q_{\min}\le q\le q_{\max}.
\]

The audit separately records lower- and upper-bound violations, rejects non-finite decisions, and recomputes performative risk from the returned decision. An infeasible solution receives infinite evaluated objective and cannot be counted as successful.

Duplicate/missing-assignment checks are not applicable because this benchmark has one scalar decision variable rather than a combinatorial assignment vector.

## Computational Cost

Decision quality is reported together with method budgets:

- `deployments`: number of environment settings at which demand is observed;
- `demand_samples`: total number of demand observations consumed by the method;
- `optimization_calls`: analytical OR solves or fixed-point solves invoked;
- `fixed_point_evaluations`: best-response residual evaluations used by the stable method.

The plug-in and stable methods are charged for all response-model intervention deployments and samples. The known-response oracle is not charged a learning budget because it is explicitly non-deployable benchmark information.

Wall-clock time is not emphasized for this tiny scalar problem because interpreter and machine overhead would dominate. Larger future benchmarks should add wall-clock and solver iteration/node statistics where they are meaningful.

## Reproducibility

All experiment controls live in JSON configuration files. Randomness uses explicit NumPy generators, and calibration sampling uses a deterministic RNG stream distinct from online deployment sampling.

Install:

```bash
python -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
python -m pip install --upgrade pip
python -m pip install -e '.[dev]'
```

Run the main benchmark:

```bash
python scripts/run_benchmark.py \
  --config configs/default.json \
  --output benchmark_results.json
```

Run the CI-sized smoke experiment:

```bash
python scripts/run_benchmark.py \
  --config configs/smoke.json \
  --output smoke_results.json
```

Run quality checks:

```bash
python -m pip check
ruff check .
ruff format --check .
mypy src
pytest --cov=performative_optimization --cov-report=term-missing --cov-fail-under=85
```

`benchmark_results.json` and `smoke_results.json` are ignored by git so a short CI run is not accidentally presented as a persistent scientific result.

## Experimental Interpretation

The scientific question is not "does ML beat OR?" The same classical newsvendor optimizer remains the decision engine inside several methods. The difference is the information supplied to it and whether deployment feedback is modeled.

Repeated optimization can approach a self-consistent point while retaining positive regret to the performative-risk oracle. Conversely, a direct-risk method can have low performative risk without being performatively stable. `stability_residual` and `performative_risk` are therefore reported separately.

A smoke run is only a software check. It must not be interpreted as scientific evidence. Meaningful conclusions require the repeated-seed benchmark and, for stronger claims, broader environment families, sensitivity analysis, and preferably real or externally validated data-generating mechanisms.

## Limitations

- The environment is synthetic and one-dimensional.
- Demand is Normal with an affine decision-dependent mean.
- Variance is decision-independent and demand is not truncated at zero; the default parameters make negative demand extremely unlikely, but the model is still an approximation.
- The response learner is linear and correctly specified in functional form for the nominal environment.
- OOD changes the response sensitivity but not the entire response family.
- There are no lead times, censoring/lost-sales inference, multi-period inventory state, strategic competitors, or multi-product coupling.
- The analytical global oracle exists because of the special affine-Normal structure; general decision-dependent stochastic programs can be nonconvex and substantially harder.
- The benchmark studies one feedback mechanism and should not be generalized to all performative systems.

## Claims Boundary

This repository demonstrates, on a controlled synthetic OR model, how a decision-dependent distribution changes optimization and how performative stability differs from direct performative-risk minimization.

It does **not** claim:

- state-of-the-art performance;
- universal superiority of performative methods;
- production readiness;
- industrial savings;
- empirical validity for a real demand system;
- reproduction of the numerical results of any cited paper;
- that a CI smoke experiment is a scientific benchmark result;
- that the analytical oracle or convergence behavior generalizes beyond the stated model.

Negative or null benchmark results should be retained rather than hidden.

## Literature Context

The literature is separated into distinct methodological strands:

1. **Foundational performative prediction.** Perdomo et al. (ICML 2020) formalize the distribution map \(\mathcal D(\theta)\) and performative stability. Mendler-Dünner et al. (NeurIPS 2020) analyze greedy and lazy stochastic deployment.
2. **Direct performative-risk optimization.** Miller et al. (ICML 2021) show why stability alone is not enough and study direct risk minimization.
3. **OR / decision-dependent stochastic optimization.** Jonsbråten, Wets, and Woodruff (1998) are an early OR reference for stochastic programs with decision-dependent random elements; Drusvyatskiy and Xiao (Mathematics of Operations Research, 2023) analyze stochastic algorithms for decision-dependent distributions. Bazotte, Carvalho, and Vidal (INFORMS Journal on Computing, 2025) study two-stage programs with endogenous uncertainty via random-variable transformation.
4. **2024 developments.** Lin and Zrnic develop plug-in performative optimization under feedback-model misspecification; Chen et al. study bandit feedback via reparameterization; Liu, Li, and Wai study two-timescale derivative-free optimization with Markovian data; Li and Wai extend stochastic optimization analysis to nonconvex performative losses.
5. **2025 developments.** Hikima, Sawada, and Fujino develop guided zeroth-order methods for unknown decision-dependent distributions. Jia et al. introduce distributionally robust performative optimization for ambiguity in the distribution map.
6. **2026 developments.** Deng and Liu study stochastic optimization with decision-dependent distributions under large delays in *Computers & Operations Research*, distinguishing stable-solution and stationary-point targets under asynchronous deployment.

These works motivate the repository's explicit separation of response-model error, deployment cost, stability, direct performative risk, and exactness.
